Recipes

Two kinds of recipe: ones that need an active starter at its peak to do the lifting, and ones that use discard purely for flavour and need no rise at all.

Honest disclaimer: I have not baked all of these

The loaf, the pancakes and the crackers are tested and in regular rotation. The rest are collected — gathered from bakers whose judgement I trust, rewritten in plain language and metric, and queued up to try. I am collecting sourdough recipes roughly the way other people collect Pokémon.

So treat an untested one as a good lead rather than a guarantee. Read it through before you start, trust your own eyes over my timings, and tell me if something is off — I will fix it and credit you.

Reading these recipes

  • Everything is in grams. Use a scale.
  • Active starter means fed, risen, and at its peak. See feeding.
  • Discard means unfed starter, straight from the jar.
  • Timings assume a kitchen at roughly 22–24 °C. Warmer is faster, cooler is slower. Watch the dough, not the clock.
  • The techniques behind the bread recipes — folds, bulk ferment, shaping, scoring, baking — are all explained on the method page.

Running out of discard?

Keep a dedicated discard jar in the fridge and tip each feed's discard into it. It keeps for a couple of weeks and builds up faster than you would think — the chocolate muffins alone will take 240 g.

Tips from other bakers

Nothing here yet — the flakes have only just gone out. As people send things back, their notes land here, with their name on them if they want it.

What is worth sending

  • Something that worked better than what is written here — a different ratio, a timing that suits a cold kitchen, a flour that behaved well.
  • Something that went wrong, and what fixed it. These are the most useful of the lot, and the hardest to find written down honestly.
  • A collected recipe you actually baked, so it can move across to tested with your name against it.
  • A photo of a loaf, good or bad. Day-three disasters especially.
