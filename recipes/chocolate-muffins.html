Discard chocolate muffins

Deep, dark and properly moist, thanks to the yoghurt. From bowl to cooling rack in under an hour, and a good way to get through a lot of discard at once.

Uses discardCollected

  • 12 muffins
  • ~40 min start to finish
  • 18–22 min at 190 °C

Wet ingredients

IngredientAmount
Sourdough discard, unfed240 g
Plain yoghurt120 g
Eggs, large2
Milk120 g
Butter, melted115 g
Vanilla extract1 tsp

Only have half that much discard? Use 120 g and keep everything else the same. The muffins are a little less tangy and every bit as good.

Dry ingredients

IngredientAmount
Plain flour220 g
Cocoa powder, unsweetened50 g
Sugar200 g
Baking powder2 tsp
Bicarbonate of soda½ tsp
Fine salt½ tsp

Optional

  • 170 g chocolate chips, plus a few for the tops
  • 50 g chopped walnuts

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 190 °C / 375 °F and line a 12-hole muffin tin.
  2. Whisk all the wet ingredients together in a large bowl until smooth.
  3. Whisk the dry ingredients together in a second bowl.
  4. Tip the dry into the wet and stir just until combined. A few streaks of flour are fine. Overmixing is the one thing that will ruin these.
  5. Fold in the chocolate chips, if using.
  6. Fill each hole about three quarters full and press a few more chips on top.
  7. Bake 18 to 22 minutes, until a skewer comes out with a few moist crumbs rather than clean.
  8. Cool in the tin for 10 minutes, then onto a rack.

Tall bakery-style tops

Start hot and then drop the heat. Bake at 220 °C for 5 minutes, then — without opening the door — reduce to 190 °C and bake another 13 to 15 minutes. The initial blast sets a dome before the crumb has finished rising.

Overnight version, for more sourdough flavour

Tonight: mix only the discard, yoghurt, flour and milk. Cover and leave on the counter for 8 to 12 hours.

Tomorrow: add everything else, mix, scoop and bake as above. The crumb comes out more tender and the flavour noticeably deeper.

Make them properly chocolatey

Add 1 tbsp of espresso powder along with the cocoa. It does not taste of coffee — it just makes the chocolate taste more like chocolate.

Keeping them

Covered at room temperature for 2 to 3 days, in the fridge for a week, or frozen for up to three months.

Collected, not yet baked by me
