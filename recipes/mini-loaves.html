Mini loaves in a muffin tin

One batch of dough, twelve small loaves, and as many different flavours as you fancy. More crust per bite than a big loaf, and the best way to test a flavour idea before committing a whole bake to it.

Needs active starterCollected

  • 12 small loaves
  • Overnight cold proof
  • 20–25 min at 230 °C

Why bother

Because a flavour idea that turns out badly costs you two rolls instead of a whole loaf you then have to eat for a week. Make a tray, try five things at once, and only commit a full loaf to the ones that earned it.

Dough

IngredientAmount
Bread flour600 g
Water420 g
Active starter, at peak120 g
Fine sea salt13 g

Makes about 1.15 kg of dough — twelve loaves of roughly 95 g, or eight larger rolls of about 140 g.

Timeline

TimeWhat
6.00 pmWhisk starter into water, add flour and salt, mix
6.30 pmStretch and fold, set 1
7.00 pmSet 2
7.30 pmSet 3
7.30–10.30 pmBulk ferment until puffy, jiggly and bubbled
10.30 pmDivide, add flavourings, shape into tight balls
11.00 pmInto a greased muffin tin, covered, into the fridge
Next dayBake straight from the fridge

A cool kitchen stretches the bulk ferment considerably. Go by the dough, not the clock — see the method.

Flavours

Amounts are per two rolls, about 190 g of dough.

FlavourAdd
Garlic parmesan25 g grated parmesan, ½ tsp garlic powder, 1 tsp chopped parsley
Rosemary parmesan20 g grated parmesan, 1 tsp chopped rosemary, black pepper
Italian herb and cheese20 g mozzarella or provolone, 1 tsp mixed dried herbs, a pinch of garlic powder
Olive and herb30–40 g chopped olives, 1 tsp mixed herbs, a pinch of garlic powder
Chocolate25 g chocolate chips, 1 tsp cocoa, 1 tbsp soft brown sugar

How to add them: flatten each piece of dough into a rough rectangle, scatter the flavouring over it, fold it over itself several times to spread it through, then shape into a tight ball.

Shaping and proofing

  1. Divide the bulk-fermented dough into 12 pieces of about 95 g.
  2. Add the flavourings and shape each into a tight ball.
  3. Sit them seam side down in a greased muffin tin, or in paper liners.
  4. Cover and refrigerate 12 to 18 hours, up to 24.

Baking

  1. Heat the oven to 230 °C / 450 °F with a baking stone or heavy tray inside.
  2. Score the tops and bake straight from the fridge.
  3. 20 to 25 minutes for 95 g loaves, 25 to 30 for 140 g ones.
  4. Done at an internal temperature of 96–99 °C / 205–210 °F.
  5. Cool on a wire rack for at least 30 minutes.

Crusty on every side

Clamp a second muffin tin upside down over the first for the first 15 minutes, then uncover and finish. It traps steam exactly the way the lid on a pot does, and you get a crisp shell the whole way round.

Keeping them

Airtight for 2 to 3 days, or frozen. Five to eight minutes at 175 °C brings them back to something very close to fresh.
