Keeping it alive

Once your starter is established it is remarkably hard to kill. Pick the routine that matches how often you actually bake, not the one that sounds most virtuous.

If you bake most weeks: the fridge

This is what most people should do.

  1. Feed the starter and let it sit at room temperature for an hour or two.
  2. Put the lid on and move it to the fridge.
  3. Feed it once a week: keep 25 g, add 25 g water and 25 g flour, leave out for an hour, back in the fridge.

Before baking, take it out and give it one or two feeds at room temperature so it is lively again. A fridge starter that has been neglected for a month usually needs two or three feeds to come back to full strength, but it will come back.

If you bake every day: the counter

Keep it at room temperature and feed once or twice a day, same ratio: 25 g starter, 25 g water, 25 g flour. Twice a day in a warm kitchen, once a day in a cool one.

Feeding ratios, and why you would change them

The three numbers describe starter : water : flour by weight.

RatioExamplePeaks inUse when
1:1:125 g + 25 g + 25 g4–6 heveryday maintenance
1:2:220 g + 40 g + 40 g6–8 hyou want it ready tomorrow morning
1:5:510 g + 50 g + 50 g10–14 hfeeding overnight, or taming a very sour starter

A bigger ratio means more food per yeast cell, so it takes longer to peak and ends up milder. A smaller ratio peaks fast and tastes sharper. That is the whole trick to controlling sourness.

Use the peak

A starter is at its strongest at its peak, the moment it stops rising and before it starts to sink. Domed top, maximum bubbles. If you mark the jar at each feed you will quickly learn your starter's timing in your kitchen, and that is more useful than any recipe's suggested timings.

Going away

  • Up to a month: feed it, refrigerate it, leave it. It will be fine.
  • Longer: dry some flakes before you go. See pass it on — the same process that got the flakes to you. Dried starter keeps for years in a cupboard and is your insurance policy.

Hydration, if you are curious

Equal weights of flour and water make a 100% hydration starter, which is the standard and what everything on this site assumes. It should look like thick batter. Stiffer starters exist and keep longer between feeds, but there is no reason to complicate your life yet.

Small habits that help

  • Scrape the sides of the jar down after feeding, so you can see the true level.
  • Keep a rubber band at the level of each fresh feed. It turns the jar into a gauge.
  • Wash the jar every couple of weeks. Dried crust on the rim is where mould starts.
  • Keep a small backup jar in the fridge, or a bag of dried flakes, so one bad week never costs you the culture.
