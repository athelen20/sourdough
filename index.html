You have a packet of flakes. Now what?

Those dry papery bits are a living sourdough culture, dehydrated and sleeping. Add flour and water, keep them warm, and in about a week you will have a bubbling starter that can raise a loaf of bread.

The short version

  1. Soak the flakes in warm water until they dissolve.
  2. Stir in an equal weight of bread flour.
  3. Keep it warm and loosely covered.
  4. Feed it once or twice a day, throwing most of it away each time.
  5. When it reliably doubles in size within about 4 to 8 hours, it is ready to bake with.

Expect 3 to 7 days. Sometimes 10. It almost always works, it just takes patience.

Meet Snow

Snow was named by my daughter. The culture came to me through a sourdough baking class run by a Portuguese baker in town, so it has travelled a bit before it reached you. I keep it on King Arthur bread flour, which is a good default if you want to match how it has been living, though it will happily adapt to whatever unbleached wheat flour you have.

A sourdough starter is just flour and water hosting a stable community of wild yeast and lactic acid bacteria. The yeast makes the gas that lifts your bread. The bacteria make the acids that give it flavour and keep unwanted microbes out. Your job is simply to feed them regularly and keep them warm enough to work.

The feeding ratios and the baking method on this site are the ones I was taught in that class, written up here in my own words so I had something to hand on with the flakes.

A note on measurements

Everything here is in grams. Sourdough is much easier when you weigh rather than scoop, because a cup of flour can vary by a third depending on how you pack it. A basic digital kitchen scale is the single best thing you can buy for this.
