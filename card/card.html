Printing these cards

Four cards per sheet, 4.25 × 5.5 inches each (a quarter of a Letter page). Page 1 is the front, page 2 is the back.

  • Print double-sided, flip on the long edge.
  • Scale: 100% - not "fit to page", or the QR code shrinks.
  • Turn on "Background graphics" in the print dialog.
  • Margins: None.
  • All four cards on a sheet are identical, so the back lines up whichever way your printer flips.
  • Cut along the dashed lines. Card stock works nicely but plain paper is fine.

Test one sheet first and scan the QR code with your phone before printing the rest.

Waking Snow up

Your packet holds 5 g of dried flakes - that is the whole batch.

You need: a digital scale reading grams, and a clean glass jar of 200-500 ml with the screw lid rested on loosely, or a cloth and rubber band.

Flour: unbleached bread flour is best; plain / all-purpose is fine. Not bleached, not self-raising.

Warm = 21-28 °C (70-82 °F). Cooler still works - the cooler it is, the longer everything takes.

  1. Day 1. Tip all 5 g of flakes into the jar with 25 g warm water. Wait 2-4 h until they dissolve, stir in 25 g flour. Cover loosely, keep warm.
  2. Day 2. Usually nothing to see yet - normal. No discarding, there is too little culture to spare: just add 25 g water + 25 g flour.
  3. Day 3. Bubbles, odd smell. Start discarding: keep 25 g, bin the rest, feed 25 g + 25 g. Day three always looks and smells alarming. Keep going.
  4. Days 4-7. Same again, every 12 hours.
Ready when it reliably doubles within 4-8 h, domes on top and smells tangy, not of solvent. Usually 5-7 days.

Now you can bake with it. Follow the instructions on the website, or your favourite baking book.

Between bakes it lives in the fridge and needs feeding about once a week. The website walks you through that, plus recipes for the loaf and for the starter you discard along the way.

Don’t panic: dark liquid on top means it’s hungry; a sharp cheesy smell is normal early on. Only bin it for fuzzy mould or pink streaks.
